about

From martensitic steel to wind turbine blades

I work at the intersection of experimental mechanics, image analysis, and probabilistic modeling — using fatigue testing, finite element simulation (ABAQUS, ANSYS), and Python-based algorithms to understand how cracks grow in real engineering structures, especially where the crack geometry factor needed for classical fracture mechanics isn't available.

My work has developed a fractal-analysis method that reads the microscopic morphology of a growing crack directly from optical images — box-counting the fractal dimension of the crack edge — to determine the crack-tip driving force without needing a closed-form geometry factor. I've extended this into a probabilistic fatigue crack growth model (based on Walker's equation, fit by maximum likelihood estimation) that accounts for uncertainty in both stress intensity range and stress ratio.

I hold an MS in Mechanical Engineering from PIEAS (Gold Medal, CGPA 3.96/4.00) and a BS from the University of Wah (Gold Medal, CGPA 3.83/4.00), and I'm currently pursuing opportunities in damage modeling for large-scale structures, including wind turbine blades.

research highlights

What I build

Multifractal crack growth model

Python · OpenCV · scikit-image

An image-analysis pipeline that reads high-resolution crack micrographs, applies OTSU thresholding and box-counting, and correlates the resulting fractal dimension with the crack-tip driving force.

Probabilistic fatigue model

Maximum likelihood estimation

A statistical extension of Walker's crack growth equation that quantifies uncertainty in ΔK and stress ratio, validated against multiple published aluminum alloy datasets.

FE validation in ABAQUS

ABAQUS · Fortran UMAT

Finite element simulations built to cross-check fractal-based predictions against classical stress-intensity solutions, including custom UMAT material subroutines.

Complex-geometry structures

L-shaped bell crank case study

Applying the fractal method to structures without a known crack geometry factor — components like compressor blades and asymmetric mechanical parts.
